# Evaluation mapping

| Evaluation question                                                                           | Prototype support                                                                                                                                                                         | Evidence collected outside the prototype                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| EQ 1.1 — Can shoppers locate and interpret appearance, quality, price and savings?            | All products are shown together; Condition B product cards and details separate the four information components.                                                                          | Participant-linked detail/choice events and task duration are stored; the moderator records answer correctness, hesitation, help requests and Microsoft Forms ratings. |
| EQ 1.2 — Are product details, prices and savings correct?                                     | One JSON record per product; integer-cent prices; savings and percentage derived in code; automated consistency check; unconfirmed records visibly marked `not verified` for researchers. | Comparison with the approved source sheet and a 3–5 participant accuracy check.                                                                                        |
| EQ 2.1 — When and why would shoppers check the label/details?                                 | Shopping-list context, product cards, optional detail opening and realistic product-choice actions support journey discussion.                                                            | Journey questionnaire and five semi-structured interviews; do not treat prototype clicks as real in-store behaviour.                                                   |
| EQ 2.2 — Does clear information and savings framing affect willingness, confidence and value? | The open-shopping demo holds product, image, quantity and current price constant; A shows basic information and B adds appearance, quality and explicit savings.                          | Compare participant-linked D1 events and completion duration alongside ratings, follow-up questions and observation notes.                                             |

## Recommended session order

1. Ask the participant to enter their name on the start screen.
2. Select the assigned label condition; every session displays the full product range.
3. Start the test only after the participant has received the approved privacy/consent information.
4. Do not explain label content before the task.
5. Record observations and answers on the approved research sheet.
6. Mark the task complete, then open the team’s Microsoft Forms questionnaire.

## Scope limitation

The prototype supports a controlled formative evaluation. It does not prove real in-store attention, actual purchasing behaviour, long-term sales effects, food-waste reduction or population-level findings.

The application stores the participant name, a random session identifier, study condition, all-product session marker, product interactions and elapsed time. It does not collect email addresses, student numbers, questionnaire answers or consent records. Researchers must handle participant names according to the approved study privacy and consent process.
